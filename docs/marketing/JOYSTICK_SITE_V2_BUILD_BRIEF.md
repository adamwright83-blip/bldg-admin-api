# JOYSTICK.com V2 — complete conversion and product-showcase rebuild
Date: 2026-10-21
Source references: user-supplied full-page screenshots of gojoystick.com and town.com; live town.com homepage reviewed 2026-10-21. This is an implementation contract, not a claim that unfinished features exist.

## The commercial goal
Sell the transformation: an ordinary business with work the owner avoids becomes an inviting, genuinely playable world where each useful move is backed by real business evidence. The customer must understand WHAT JOYSTICK does, WHY it's fun, how the agents and companions help, what exists today, what requires approval, and what it costs. User standard: premium, cohesive, charming, investor-grade, never funnel SaaS, Pixar pet, 3D isometric toy village, cheesy sticky note, fake functionality, fabricated metrics, or generic animated AI art. Light mode only.

## Non-negotiable quality
- Ship 10–12 distinct meaningful editorial sections plus a real footer, equal to or more complete than the referenced Town homepage. Town structure analyzed: hero; helpful from hello; product/work examples/integrations; repeat-work routines; collaborative team; trust & safety; character roster/CTA; solution navigations; closing brand/footer. DO NOT COPY Town's shapes, animations, mascots, quotes, content, or layout. Match completeness and conversion logic, not imitation.
- Every section presents a specific premise, visible proof or interaction, and next-step or payoff. No vague AI prose or large blank bands. Large editorial headlines with excellent typography; strong readable supporting text (minimum 16px typical desktop, 15px mobile) and visible CTAs/microcopy. Preserve considerable whitespace but remove enormous contentless gaps.
- Existing Rook, Sable, Luma, Orren line drawing personalities/identities should remain consistent. Reframe them in elevated collectible field cards, not taped Post-its. No visible tape, yellow square, sticky paper gimmick or faceless mock photos. Each persona has an independent contrasting color/texture: Adam brass-ivory, Daisy soft rose, Sean sea glass, TJ burnt clay/lilac. Color palette is a starting art direction, not rigid implementation.
- Human caption verb tenses in PRESENT. Rotate personas with accessible direct selection, pause on hover/focus, independent fade timer, reduced-motion handling. Phone card flips on keyboard/tap and mouse. Avoid flip actions that reveal bracketed placeholders, inauthentic photographs, or faux game results.
- CTA everywhere: "Turn my work into a game →" (primary). Supporting line must be visually readable, e.g., "Answer three questions. See a personalized preview. No account needed." Only claim no-account if actual preview route works. Verify routes and be explicit if it's sample-data-only. No pretend checkout; actual checkout remains intentionally closed pending Stripe/entitlement verification.
- The website must not claim agents performed actions that were not actually supported or independently evidenced. Fictional examples are labeled illustrative; screenshots must come from actual playable product builds. No fabricated testimonials, return, conversion multipliers, autonomous send, SOC2, or phone capabilities beyond verified feature scope.

## Landing narrative, full section-by-section implementation

1. HERO — An unexpected category.
   Headline should retain the winning idea: "Knows your business. / Turns your next move into a game." Alternative candidate for A/B: "The monsters are fake. The work is real." Use a main pitch that a first-time visitor can understand, not just poetic language.
   Four rotating portrait/persona canvases, refined editorial print-object cards rather than Post-its; hover or tap reveals a meaningful proof surface if verified. Small human name/caption readable.
   Adam: "Adam loves Tomb Raider. Approaching a front desk is the level he'd rather skip."
   Daisy: "Daisy loves cozy games. Keeping up with customers? Not so cozy."
   Sean (change spelling from Shawn): "Sean doomscrolls IG & TikTok. He has ad-creation phobia."
   TJ: "TJ plays Call of Duty every night. Door hangers on strangers' front doors? He'd rather respawn."
   Human front is a truthful portrait/illustrative treatment, NOT a promised real photo placeholder. On hover a micro storyboard connects avoidance -> move -> game. Primary CTA above fold and legible proof/no-account message; repeat anchored CTA at each decisive section.
   Do not claim these four represent actual customer testimonials.

2. CLAIRE — Seeing the work / producing the next move.
   Headline: "Claire reads your [books / CRM / customer messages / goals]." A refined animated word turnover is optional, subject to verified integration/first-party evidence. The verbs must work for each term, reduced motion shows a static default and no layout shift. Accessible live announcements or do not announce every automatic cycle.
   Standfirst: "She sees the detail that gets lost between orders, customers, and the day you meant to have. Then she helps you choose what to do about it."
   Proof interaction: single beautiful compact two-state card. State A "What Claire noticed": 8 customers absent from recent orders; source, observation time, unknowns. State B on flip "What becomes playable": one proposed review-ready action on the real Day Line and a neighborhood/mission preview. NO claim of real Maya data without evidence. "She calls. You don't type." only if actual scheduled calls are verified available for paying customer. Do not misrepresent drafts as sent or mark anticipated earnings as captured.
   Tease Claire's personality sparingly: one sly line such as "She knows your business. She's less forthcoming about herself." Do not promise lore unlocks the app does not ship.

3. COMPANIONS — Make the category obvious.
   Headline: "Meet your companions. They're good at the parts you avoid." Standfirst: "Four helpful oddballs. Real strengths. Terrible habits. You meet them through work that actually gets done."
   Four tall collectible editorial cards preserving existing line drawings, with distinct colors, beautiful typography and small dynamic funny thought bubbles; cards flip on hover/tap/keyboard to a documented game moment or clearly labeled illustrative vignette, not an unverified autonomous result. Give clear job + defect on every card:
   Rook — "Walks into the lobby like he owns the place." Flaw: "His opening pitch includes a yacht he has never seen." Bubble "I told them we're practically royalty." Claire's user-facing actual copy must never be exaggerated.
   Sable — "Knows exactly who needs a follow-up." Flaw: "Has a memory like an elephant and the bedside manner of a filing cabinet." Bubble "The one in West Hollywood. The dog. The rug. You remember." Avoid suggesting production loses customers' names; this is character dialogue, not data contract.
   Luma — "Gets the ad concept off your someday list." Flaw: "By version eleven, the others hide her laptop." Bubble "One last revision." If actual ad creation is not live, label a gameplay/illustrative example, not posted marketing.
   Orren — "Finds the next useful stop." Flaw: "He's never taken a wrong turn. According to Orren." Bubble "That was scenic routing."
   Back of every card: one specific business move, sample game event and proof/approval state. No direct claim that Rook dials, Luma publishes, or Orren books unsupervised. Avoid monotonous mirrored 4-card SaaS row; staged interaction/reveal with responsive fallback.

4. THE GAME — The work you put off is the level you want to play.
   Headline: "Work worth doing. A world worth returning to." Visual: genuine launch-quality footage of Silver Lake mission, real Day Line/action to visible world state. Describe exactly which actions the player takes and what evidence advances the world. One gorgeous lead scene + three smaller scenes, with context. Real game only; no fake UI, AI-generated replacement stills marketed as product footage, raw prototype captures promoted as finished, broken video or bracketed placeholders.
   Current screenshots for Silver Lake, Beverly Hills, Channel 382, Century Park East are NOT accepted quality. See separate game art gate.

5. FIRST FIVE MINUTES — Town's "Helpful from hello" parallel.
   Headline: "No blank workspace. No fifty-step setup." Demonstrate actual onboarding: three questions -> truthful preview (clearly sample or personalized) -> choose a first action -> optional authorized integrations after first value. If the app does not support the full path, show the verified parts and "In preview" appropriately. A short interactive choice (business type and pain point) may transform a real static sample preview, not simulate a backend result.

6. REPEAT WORK — Town's routines parallel.
   Headline: "The follow-up doesn't have to live in your head." Show the Day Line as the same mission across day, Claire call, driver action, and review. Repeating work: an overdue customer, a building to visit, a route to revisit; demonstrate approvals, edits, due dates and outcome unknown. No automatic SMS send. Show a scheduled sequence only if currently shipped and reliable, otherwise upcoming.

7. A WHOLE CREW — Town's collaboration parallel.
   Headline: "You run the business. The world comes with a crew." Clear role architecture: Claire (chief of staff / briefings and decisions), Daphne (operator context/explicit preferences with consent), Mitch (game creator — concept/beta if unfinished), President (governed business operator — preview if unfinished), companions (in-game actors, not additional autonomous identities). Visual relationships as scene from a playable office/world, not organization chart, chatbot roster, or SaaS card collage. Explain human approval and work continuity; omit teams/cross-persona collaboration claims that aren't validated.

8. TRUST / CONTROL — Town's security parallel.
   Headline: "Nothing leaves the game without your say-so." Art: evidence -> proposed move -> review/approval -> execution evidence -> world reaction. Product-specific trust: approved actions, idempotency, tenant isolation, data freshness, consent, known/unknown status. For each step show one real outcome/receipt example. Distinguish game monsters and fiction from business truth. Never claim independently audited security certification unless verified. Footer links privacy, terms and contact must be real.

9. PROGRESSION / WORLD STORY — uniquely JOYSTICK, not a Town clone.
   Headline: "Your world changes when you do." Contrast two actual in-game states across a verified real-world action; no coins, XP, streaks, trophies, synthetic revenue, forced daily play. The city remembers real actions; fiction/companions can have mystery and personality. Claire's private backstory teaser should be short and not claim features behind a fictitious "earned intimacy" gate. Real evidence animates the world; it does not purchase social pressure.

10. PRICING — sophisticated packaging, NOT a stealth billing change.
   IMPORTANT commercial conflict: deployed landing currently shows 7-day trial at $49/month (older approved live billing hypothesis). October 20 pricing work proposed Explore free/no-card, JOYSTICK $79/month per workspace with $40/month controlled AI work allowance and game unmetered, Commerce $119/month (proposed native commerce package), and separately approved Workshop/Mitch/Prez custom builds (illustrative minimum top-up $20). These prices/allowances are NOT verified approved checkout SKUs or live backend entitlements. Earlier $468/year and 7-day card-required trial are older configuration, not silently compatible with no-card Explore and monthly-only newer proposal.
   Build polished pricing architecture with 3 visual offers: Explore (free sample / no card only when verified), JOYSTICK (proposed $79/month), Commerce (proposed $119/month), with Workshop as clearly scoped pay-for-approved-build section, NOT an unbounded included AI promise. Candidate pricing detail: unlimited gameplay/navigation/saved work reads; governed/metered AI work with transparent included allowance; additional spends OFF by default; customer approves top-ups and each Workshop scope; commerce is native website/checkout/service operations only when validated — NOT a template funnel builder, misleading POS or unsupported payments.
   **PUBLISH GATE:** do not expose $79/$119/$40/$20 as a payable live commercial offer or bind it to checkout until founder accepts updated pricing AND server plan entitlements/usage meters & Stripe products match. Until then use a genuine "Explore the preview" and/or "Request early access" conversion path; do not bury the old $49 legal/current terms under a new price. Avoid dead-end pricing CTA or false free-trial checkout.

11. FAQ / OBJECTIONS — direct not defensive.
   "Is this a game or business software?" Both, but real customer/orders/payments only when backed by connected/entered evidence.
   "Does JOYSTICK contact my customers?" Nothing sent without consent/approval; state actual device-assisted handoff.
   "What can I do before connecting anything?" Sample world / preview, three questions, choose first action — only if genuine.
   "What counts toward AI usage?" Transparent metered work; no unspecified unlimited access.
   "Can my team use it?" Honest role/capability limits.
   "Do I own my data?" Describe actual export/deletion and privacy conditions, not aspirational rights.
   "Can I cancel?" Accurate agreement and billing, no false promise.
   "Will it automatically increase sales?" No guarantee; makes verified opportunities and next actions easier to see and pursue.

12. CLOSING / BRAND — conversion and personality.
   Headline: "The next level is the one you've been putting off." CTA: "Turn my work into a game →". Underneath: legible, honest first-step text and transparent availability. Footer: Product, Play, How it works, Trust, Pricing, FAQ, Sign in, Terms, Privacy, Contact (only working routes). Can close with one small mischievous companion line as distinctive payoff, not giant filler brand wallpaper.
