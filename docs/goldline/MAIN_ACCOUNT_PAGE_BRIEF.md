# JOYSTICK — Main Account Page Creative Brief (v2)

**This document constrains future main-account-page work. Current production/main outranks prose.**

**Status:** v2, 2026-10-27. Concept by Adam. Additions by Claude, approved by Adam in conversation the same day.
**Spoilers:** none here. The repository is public. Season answers (what is on the floor, who Claire is, why she cares
about the building) live outside the repo and are never committed.
**Supersedes:** the v1 brief pasted in conversation on 2026-10-27. Everything in v1 is kept except where this document
says otherwise.

---

## 0. The one sentence

The operator arrives to check on their business and stays because they want to know what happens next.

The page has one job: get the operator to play ONE game, for about five minutes, tonight.

---

## 1. The concept

JOYSTICK is escapism from the business, not the business dressed as a game. The business supplies the stakes, the
snapshot and the currency. The game itself is somewhere else.

Rather than a dashboard, a task list and an entire city of games, the main account page shows:

1. a quick, truthful business snapshot;
2. Claire, mid-mystery, holding one piece of story back;
3. one PLAY button for tonight's chapter;
4. the floating dream board above the city;
5. the neighbourhood strip along the bottom, revenue per neighbourhood, lit only where the story has been.

A prominent **+ New Order** button stays reachable at all times.

---

## 2. The page

### 2a. Business snapshot (top)

Light mode. Week-to-date and month-to-date revenue, orders and new customers, each against the prior period. These
are real numbers from the operator's books. Nothing in this strip is ever fictional.

### 2b. Tonight's chapter (hero)

Chapter number, title, Claire's before-line (see §4), one PLAY control, and three honest facts about the session:
expected minutes, orders to complete inside the game if any, follow-ups it will produce if any.

### 2c. The dream board (above the city)

See §7.

### 2d. The neighbourhood strip (bottom)

Miniature 3D neighbourhoods with monthly revenue and customer count. Only neighbourhoods the story has visited are
lit. The rest are present but dim. See §6.

---

## 3. Two Claire modes (hard rule)

Claire has two voices and the operator can always tell which one is speaking.

| Mode | Where | May say | May never say |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Business** | calls, Claire Desk, snapshot, Day Line, debriefs | anything with a receipt; "I don't know" | anything from the story |
| **Story** | the hero before-line, the after-line, inside a chapter | authored fiction about fictional people, floors, cameras, keys | an invented customer, order, revenue figure, meeting or outcome |

Fiction may anchor to real places and real hours (Century Park East, 2:14 AM, the lobby camera, 21 floors). It never
names a real customer or claims a real business event. The existing truth guards on Business-mode Claire are
untouched by this brief.

The two modes cross exactly once per season (§7d). Never otherwise.

---

## 4. The nightly chapter

Pressing PLAY runs a three-part experience.

1. **Before:** Claire's before-line, one beat longer than the hero line, then the chapter starts.
2. **Game:** one five-minute game inside Lantern City with its own genre, characters and mechanics. Any existing
   neighbourhood game is eligible as a chapter (Century Park East's monitor wall, Silver Lake's courtyard, Beverly
   Hills, CHANNEL 382) once it has a five-minute cut.
3. **After:** Claire's after-line, then one question to the player, then back to the page with the next chapter's
   title visible but locked until tomorrow.

### 4a. Before-lines

- A specific, small, impossible thing. A button, a timestamp, a count. Never a theme or an omen.
- Three lines at most.
- Claire withholds. The line ends on the thing she stops short of saying.
- Carries one real anchor: a real building, a real hour, a real camera, a real floor count.

Illustrative only (not Season 1 canon):

> Last night at 2:14 someone pressed the button for 22. Century Park East has 21 floors. The elevator went up
> anyway. I have the lobby footage. I don't have footage of where it stopped.

### 4b. After-lines

- Answers one small question. Opens a bigger one.
- Every third chapter reframes something from an earlier chapter, so the player feels the season was built, not
  improvised.
- Ends with Claire asking the player one question. The answer is stored and becomes canon. Claire does not react
  to it that night.

### 4c. Decisions are the cliffhanger

The player's answer is the cliffhanger. The next before-line opens from it. Each chapter authors two branches off
the previous night's answer; branches converge at the mid-season turn so authoring cost stays fixed per season.
Prior answers are durable game state and are never shown as a meter.

### 4d. You cannot lose a chapter

A chapter can only be finished slower. The after-line always plays. A clean win adds one extra line Claire would
not otherwise say. A tired operator at 11pm never gets a wasted night.

### 4e. Missed nights

No streak is lost and nothing is shamed. The story kept moving. The next before-line acknowledges the gap in
fiction ("Three nights. The button lit every one of them. I counted."). Absence is itself a hook.

### 4f. One-line recaps

Claire never recaps more than one line ("You left the doorman with the key.").

## 5. The Day 2 reveal

Claire removes the fox mask on Day 2 and is a human woman from then on. This stays from v1. Three additions:

- **The player triggers it.** Whatever the player answers on Night 1, Night 2 opens with Claire deciding they have
  earned her face. Same reveal, but caused by the player, so the player owns it.
- **It reconciles two surfaces that already exist.** The operator has already heard Claire's human voice on calls.
  The page showed a fox. Day 2 is the voice on the phone getting a face. The next day's call lands differently.
- **The mask moves.** Later in the season a figure on a monitor is wearing it. Claire says nothing. The reveal opened
  a question; it did not close one.

Who Claire is and why she cares about the building is season-level content and is not written here.

---

## 6. The story paces the city

Chapter 01 is set in one neighbourhood. Each subsequent chapter moves because the story moves (a key card leads to
Silver Lake, a name on a deed leads to Beverly Hills). Each chapter lights one neighbourhood on the strip.

This is how the city is revealed. There is no tutorial, no lock icon and no "unlock all" path. The strip is the
season's table of contents. The operator never sees the whole city until the story has been everywhere.

---

## 7. The dream board

### 7a. What it is

An enormous translucent board hanging above Lantern City. The operator names a dream in onboarding (family, a home,
financial freedom, or their own). The board is one round toward that dream. Squares light from completed Day Line
items and verified business missions. The board is cinematic and high-stakes, in deliberate contrast with the cozy
worlds below.

### 7b. What a round means

Completing one round is meant to be a measurable change in the operator's life, because every lit square was a
real thing they did. The board gives the nightly ritual its structure: the story is why they come to the door each
night; the board is what makes the coming count.

### 7c. Real work is the diamond

Vertical dramas sell diamonds to skip the wait for the next episode. JOYSTICK's wait is natural: one chapter per
night. The only way to open the next chapter early is a newly lit square on the board. The fiction stays fiction.
The business is the wallet, never the plot.

### 7d. One sanctioned crossing per season

The season finale is written to land the night the round completes. That night, and only that night, Business-mode
Claire says one line on the real call that only means something if the operator watched the story. Once per season.
Never elsewhere.

### 7e. The board is silent in the story

Story-mode Claire never mentions the board. It lights on its own schedule.

---

## 8. Seasons

- One season is one dream-board round: nine to twelve chapters, a mid-season turn where Claire's own thread surfaces,
  a finale paced to the board.
- Seasons are authored, finite content. The writers' room is a fixed cost per season, not per night.
- Next season: next round, new starting neighbourhood, same Claire.
- Season answers are kept outside the repository.

---

## 9. The hypothesis

Replacing an open-ended dashboard with a focused, serialized nightly chapter is expected to raise: the share of
operators who press PLAY, minutes played, chapter completion, and return frequency. This is a hypothesis to measure,
not a result. Measure each against the current page before and after. Do not describe the mechanism as
gamification; it is an initiation system with serialized story.

---

## 10. Do not

- Do not replace the story with "real findings", receipts, lapsed customers or any version of making the business the
  plot. The reunion quests inside Silver Lake remain a game mechanic; they are not the hero.
- Do not put a real customer, order or revenue figure into Story-mode text.
- Do not let Business-mode Claire reference the story outside §7d.
- Do not add streaks, losses, shame or visible meters for story state.
- Do not commit season answers, the reveal's backstory or the finale to this repository.
- Do not show the whole city on first open.
