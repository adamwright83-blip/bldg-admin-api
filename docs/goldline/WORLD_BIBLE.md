**This document constrains future Goldline work. Current production/main outranks prose. Do not rebuild systems that already satisfy these laws.**

# DOCUMENT 1 — GOLDLINE WORLD BIBLE v1.1

**Audience:** writers, artists, character designers, level designers, combat designers, trailer/marketing creative.
**Purpose:** make Goldline worth playing.
**Hard rule:** no SaaS vocabulary. No CRM vocabulary. No internal agent architecture.

## 1. Premise

> **Seven people knew how to make the world grow. Each broken kingdom is holding one of them prisoner.**

The world was once continuous.

Then came **the Sunder**.

Mountains lifted into the sky. Seas remained suspended around drowned cities. Roads ended in open air. Rivers fell sideways. Pieces of highways reappeared embedded vertically in distant mountains. Enormous roots remained stretched between islands that once touched.

The seven people who understood how new roads appeared were separated.

Centuries later, each surviving kingdom is keeping one of them from the others in its own way.

Some are imprisoned.

Some exploited.

Some hunted.

Some trapped by bargains.

Some have escaped but cannot truly leave.

Some may not want to be found.

Trailblazer moves through the broken world freeing them.

Every person she recovers permanently changes the adventure.

---

# 2. Player fantasy

Goldline is a **cinematic mobile action-adventure**.

The immediate fantasy is:

* be a dangerous, capable female adventurer;
* physically move through spectacular places;
* fight memorable enemies;
* dodge;
* parry;
* use the Linehook;
* discover routes;
* survive set pieces;
* collect Relics;
* reach things that looked unreachable;
* steal an enormous skyship;
* gradually fill that ship with people, creatures, objects and history.

Some Gold Lines continue after the fantasy world itself ends.

Whatever happens beyond them can return and alter the world.

Do not overexplain this early.

---

# 3. Genre and presentation law

Goldline is designed for an audience that responds to **cinematic character-forward action-adventure**, not cute fantasy or Nintendo-coded exploration.

The intended sensation is:

> **I am standing behind this woman and I am about to go somewhere dangerous.**

Reference family:

* Tomb Raider
* Uncharted
* premium third-person action-adventure
* accessible mobile action combat
* cinematic expedition structure

Goldline is not:

* Zelda-like
* puzzle-shrine-first
* isometric RPG
* JRPG
* side-scroller
* top-down fantasy
* cozy fantasy
* tiny-character-over-map
* productivity RPG

### Playable presentation law

The implementation may use painted 2D environments and sprites.

**The player must not perceive the implementation as a flat 2D game.**

Environment art should normally extend beyond the portrait viewport.

The phone is a **camera into a larger stage**.

Trailblazer must remain large and instantly readable.

Future stages should use:

* oversized painterly environments;
* authored walkable corridors/polygons;
* perspective scaling;
* moving camera;
* depth;
* foreground occlusion;
* live enemies/interactables;
* transition gates;
* environmental motion.

Never solve an implementation problem by simply zooming out until the entire level fits on-screen.

If a paused gameplay frame reads as “2D fantasy game board,” the composition has failed.

# 4. Trailblazer

No prophecy.

No royal blood.

No “chosen savior.”

Trailblazer is an adventurer.

Her useful personality flaw is that she becomes curious at exactly the moment sensible people become cautious.

A sign says:

**DO NOT ENTER**

She wonders why.

A mechanism is shaking violently.

She touches it.

Someone says nobody has survived a route.

She starts checking her equipment.

Her humor is primarily literal rather than quippy.

Rook:

> “Plan?”

Trailblazer:

> “Yes.”

> “Good.”

> “Go away from it quickly.”

But she must also be allowed awe.

When she finally reaches something she has spent hours looking toward, she does not always make a joke.

Sometimes she just looks.

---

# 5. Her first desire

Do not begin with:

**SAVE THE WORLD.**

Above the clouds is an enormous legendary skyship:

# THE WAYWARD

It has been tethered in place for generations.

Nobody remembers seeing it sail.

Stories about it contradict one another.

Trailblazer's reaction is uncomplicated.

> **She wants it.**

The early adventure grows outward from that desire.

The larger world mystery catches her afterward.

---

# 6. The Cut

During an old Gold Line accident, a tiny filament entered Trailblazer's forearm.

It never came out.

It moves subtly beneath her skin like a splinter that refuses to surface.

This is:

# THE CUT

Normally it is almost dormant.

Near old Line machinery:

warm.

Near an active Line:

bright.

Near a place where the Line leaves the world:

painful.

Sometimes it physically pulls beneath the skin toward something Trailblazer cannot see.

Do not turn it into constant glowing-chosen-one decoration.

Use it sparingly and physically.

---

# 7. The Gold Line

A physical filament thinner than wire and bright as molten metal.

It can run:

* through stone;
* under roads;
* across ship hulls;
* through water;
* along structures;
* into open air.

Nobody can manufacture it.

Nobody can simply command it.

An old children's saying survives:

> **The Line cannot be lied to.**

Do not repeat this constantly.

Children say it.

Crooks mock it.

Sable takes it more seriously than most adults.

### Visual states

**Dormant:** hair-thin seam.

**Waking:** nearby dust begins lifting toward it.

**Active:** bright filament ignores ordinary surface geometry.

**Creating:** the missing structure first appears as luminous architectural wireframe.

Then matter arrives violently.

Stone slams into place.

Vines lash around supports.

Lanterns ignite.

Water changes direction.

The Gold Line's reward language is **physical wonder**.

---

# 8. The Needle, the Sunder and Elsewhere

Long ago the old world constructed:

# THE NEEDLE

Its purpose was to discover where the Gold Line went when it passed beyond known geography.

Something happened when it was activated.

Then came:

# THE SUNDER

Nobody now possesses the entire truth.

Different kingdoms maintain contradictory explanations.

Do not resolve this immediately.

Where the Line finally disappears is informally called:

# ELSEWHERE

Nobody defines Elsewhere correctly.

Possible theories heard in-world:

* another country;
* the land of the dead;
* where unfinished things go;
* where lost socks go;
* inside the Needle;
* nowhere;
* a second world.

Some sailors claim they have heard impossible mechanical sounds through dormant Lines.

The game does not explain those sounds for them.

---

# 9. The broken ring

A gigantic broken golden ring remains visible across much of the sky.

It is associated with the Needle.

It should become one of Goldline's iconic skyline elements.

At certain moments, another tiny segment activates.

The player should spend a long time thinking:

> **I'm eventually going up there.**

---

# 10. The Wayward

The Wayward is not a menu.

It is a place.

And eventually:

**home.**

Initially it is enormous, empty and cold.

Footsteps echo.

Rooms serve no purpose.

Then the people Trailblazer encounters begin changing it.

Mara covers walls and ceilings with maps.

Bront takes the galley.

Luma alters architecture without permission.

Sable keeps a room almost nobody enters.

Ilex sleeps near the hull because she likes listening to the ship.

Orren rearranges things to reduce walking time.

Rook somehow knows every dockworker at every port.

Mission damage remains visible.

Objects accumulate.

Souvenirs appear.

Animals sneak aboard.

A campaign should ideally leave **some permanent physical consequence aboard the Wayward**.

It might be:

* a person;
* an animal;
* structural damage;
* a plant;
* stolen machinery;
* a ridiculous souvenir;
* a new room;
* an artifact;
* a local custom somebody brought aboard.

The ship gradually becomes the physical history of playing Goldline.

After a bad expedition, the emotional payoff may be nothing more than:

Trailblazer boards.

Bront pushes food toward her.

Someone is playing music above deck.

The wood creaks.

No exposition.

# 11. The Seven

## Mara Vale

Cartographer.

Refuses to draw coastlines.

She believes edges teach people where to stop looking.

Her maps become less finished near their boundaries.

Eventually her cabin has no free wall space and she sleeps underneath the table.

Trailblazer:

> “Where does this go?”

Mara:

> “Somewhere.”

> “Useful.”

> “You asked where.”

She notices routes, omissions and places everyone else overlooked.

---

## Sable Quill

Cannot forget.

Not figuratively.

Everything remains.

Words.

Dates.

Mistakes.

Faces.

Sounds.

Someone breaks a cup early in the game.

Twenty hours later:

> “You broke one exactly like that in the Graft.”

Trailblazer:

> “Could you forget that?”

Sable:

> “No.”

Her ability is valuable and awful.

---

## Rook Venn

Former infamous liar.

After the Sunder, whenever Rook attempts a direct lie, his voice simply fails.

Unfortunately, this did nothing to reduce how much he talks.

Social.

Fearless.

Curious.

Charming.

Good with strangers.

Must work inside truth.

---

## Bront Mercer

Enormous.

Looks like he should be the party's brute.

Instead he cooks.

Major negotiations become meals.

He watches what people eat.

A ruler threatens execution while scraping the final sauce from his plate.

Bront:

> “He likes the offer.”

Trailblazer:

> “He's going to hang us.”

> “Different problem.”

---

## Ilex

Nearly blind.

Experiences much of the world through resonance.

Speech produces perceptible geometry.

Buildings have frequencies.

Machines have rhythm.

People sound different when their attention or intention changes.

She cannot magically detect truth.

She notices patterns other people miss.

---

## Luma Fenn

Cannot leave anything alone.

Every time Trailblazer comes home:

new sail.

Painted cannon.

Hole cut between decks.

Trapdoor.

Rope swing through Bront's kitchen.

Eventually an entire room nobody authorized.

Compulsively inventive.

---

## Orren Pike

Navigator.

Hates maps.

His argument:

> “A map becomes obsolete the moment you finish it.”

Reads:

* wind;
* traffic;
* crowds;
* clouds;
* animals;
* sound;
* changing conditions.

Constant ideological fight with Mara.

Mara:

> “The road is here.”

Orren:

> “Was.”

---

# 12. Do not reveal the Seven mechanically

Do not repeat:

kingdom → boss → prison opens → person joins.

Their captivity should take different forms.

Current preferred possibilities:

* Mara is genuinely imprisoned.
* Sable initially hunts Trailblazer.
* Rook is rumored captured but is operating an illegal communications network.
* Bront refuses to join.
* Ilex travels anonymously before her identity becomes clear.
* Luma is encountered sabotaging things long before formal recruitment.
* Orren escaped and does not want the Seven reunited.

The premise remains simple.

The route to satisfying it should not be predictable.

# 13. Kingdom One — Brass Republic / Colosseum

The Brass Republic believes **certainty is civilization**.

Saying:

> “I don't know.”

is considered indecent.

Therefore everyone confidently answers questions they cannot possibly answer.

### Cultural insanity

Professional **Hesitators** are hired to stand beside wealthy people and pause dramatically on their behalf.

Restaurants publicly score every meal.

Funerals reenact arguments the deceased **lost**.

Children study dramatic entrances.

Audience balconies exist in places that have no legitimate reason to contain audiences.

The Colosseum contains six ceremonial doors.

Door Six has been debated for generations.

Books written.

Cults formed.

Careers destroyed.

There is nothing behind it.

The actual passage is an ugly service entrance beneath the arena.

---

# 14. Clockhead / Vellum Kai

Huge astronomical clock mechanism integrated into his body/head.

Bronze.

Stone.

Moving rings.

Readable instantly.

His obsession:

> Nothing should happen before the correct time.

The problem:

the correct time never seems to arrive.

Clocks throughout his world may read:

* SOON
* PENDING
* AFTER REVIEW
* NEXT WEEK
* WHEN CONDITIONS IMPROVE
* PROVISIONALLY
* NOT YET

Some have no hands.

Do not explain his psychological meaning.

Players should first experience:

**this asshole will not let anything happen.**

### Combat language

**Aimed Bolt** — hangs in place before suddenly resuming.

**Clock Fan** — multiple projectiles traveling on staggered timing.

**Second Hand** — huge spectral sweep.

**Borrowed Minute** — Clockhead rewinds while his previous attack continues.

**Deferred Damage** — attack appears resolved, then impacts later.

**Stolen Second** — temporarily interferes with Trailblazer's defensive timing.

His mechanics should express him before dialogue does.

---

# 15. Great Tree / The Graft

A tree large enough to contain villages on individual branches.

Its hollow trunk becomes a vertical world.

Roots vanish into cloud.

At adolescence, citizens are **Grafted** to a branch-community.

That branch becomes their family identity.

Sometimes the tree rejects somebody.

Those people become:

# THE UNGRAFTED

They live in illegal rope settlements suspended between branches.

Officially, they do not exist.

Naturally, their neighborhoods should be among the most visually interesting.

### Required social weirdness

Each future kingdom must contain at least **three memorable cultural details unrelated to combat mechanics**.

For the Great Tree, possible examples include:

* branch families competing over whose sap-fermented drink is least disgusting;
* marriage ceremonies requiring both partners to sleep suspended in a rope net overnight;
* branch residents refusing to use ladders built by rival branches;
* Ungrafted children giving themselves deliberately absurd surnames.

### Boss

# THE MOUTH BELOW

For much of the level, players see roots moving.

Eventually they discover some are not roots.

A second parasitic network has learned to imitate the tree.

It creates fake traversal structures.

One bridge collapses.

One moves.

One bites.

---

# 16. The Beanstalk

This level must escalate absurdity repeatedly.

Every fifteen minutes:

> **What the hell is up here now?**

Lower:

recognizable plant climbing.

Higher:

gravity rotates ninety degrees.

A tavern is built sideways.

Rain falls horizontally.

Higher:

cloud fishermen cast enormous lines into storms.

Higher:

homes from an old island were carried upward by the growing stalk.

Higher:

people who have never touched ordinary ground.

Higher:

Cloudjaw.

### Cloudjaw

Looks like another floating island from a distance.

Then opens an eye.

Eventually Trailblazer discovers it has been restrained rather than naturally hostile.

She cuts it free.

Much later:

huge shadow crosses the Wayward.

Everybody looks up.

Cloudjaw.

No quest marker.

No pop-up.

No reward.

The world remembers.

---

# 17. Mooring City

A civilization built around maintaining an enormous ship that is legally forbidden to sail.

Nobody fully remembers why.

Entire professions evolved around it:

* rope inspectors;
* sail archivists;
* mast polishers;
* anchor priests;
* hull historians.

The absurdity should feel culturally normal to its inhabitants.

### Tether Engine

The fortress holding the ship becomes the encounter.

Winches.

Ropes.

Chains.

Moving deck sections.

Anchor weapons.

Trailblazer is dismantling the system holding the Wayward rather than simply attacking one enormous HP sponge.

Eventually the ship moves for the first time in generations.

That sequence should be a major campaign payoff.

---

# 18. Crystal Chasm / Facet Court

Crystal bends:

* light;
* distance;
* perspective;
* memory.

Players may see enemies before they actually arrive.

Reflections may show Trailblazer taking another route.

Locals believe no single interpretation is sufficient.

Legal disputes can continue for decades.

Sable despises the place.

### Prism Regent

Multiple visible bodies.

All can harm Trailblazer.

Only one is currently vulnerable.

Which one changes according to environmental reflection geometry.

Crystal Chasm should become a showcase for **Trace**, **Recall**, Linehook movement and luminous Gold-vs-crystal readability.

---

# 19. Flooded City

An entire drowned city suspended inside an enormous sphere of ocean.

From outside, players can see architecture and fish through the water.

Inside:

* underwater streets;
* air chambers;
* bell towers;
* inverted waterfalls;
* ruins;
* pressure systems.

Traversal changes from ordinary movement to diving, surfacing, currents and changing water state.

### Bell Beneath

The submerged cathedral is part of the boss encounter.

Bells alter water.

Wrong sequence can flood areas.

Correct sequence reveals routes.

---

# 20. Jungle of Old Roads

The old transport capital swallowed by jungle.

This is one of the most kinetic environments.

Visual ingredients:

* wooden ramps;
* rope bridges;
* vines;
* ziplines;
* counterweights;
* collapsed roads;
* buried infrastructure;
* giant route markers;
* vertical shortcuts.

Bright.

Fast.

Dangerous.

### Great Run

No conventional boss required.

The ancient road rebuilds while Trailblazer is moving.

Stone segments assemble ahead.

Other pieces rotate.

Water erupts through ruins.

Enemies pursue.

Bridge fails.

Linehook.

Swing.

Jump.

Road materializes beneath her.

A major trailer sequence.

# 21. Creature ecology

Enemies should not exist solely because a combat room needed five mobs.

Creatures have wants.

Cloud Kites hunt.

Sap Thieves steal from enemies as well as Trailblazer.

Road Apes can use traversal systems and accidentally reveal shortcuts.

Mimic Fruit sometimes genuinely is fruit.

Predators may attack prey.

Territorial creatures may ignore the player until provoked.

Some animals eventually board the Wayward.

A creature changing its behavior because of a previous campaign is preferable to a reward popup explaining that the world changed.

# 22. Pip

Name provisional.

Small Gold-Line-adjacent creature.

Mischievous.

Steals things.

Sleeps where it should not.

Can sometimes stand on Gold Lines Trailblazer cannot yet use.

May bite Clockhead.

Eventually lives aboard the Wayward.

Pip must never become an exposition mascot.

He does not explain missions.

He occasionally seems aware of Elsewhere in ways nobody understands.

# 23. Combat

Trailblazer's signature weapon:

# LINEBLADE

A broken sword hilt containing exposed Gold Line.

No conventional blade.

Swinging it creates a temporary luminous edge through space.

### Core verbs

* slash
* heavy attack
* dodge
* parry
* shield
* Linehook
* contextual interaction
* contextual traversal

### TRACE

Successful motion/combat can leave temporary Gold Line geometry in space.

### RECALL

Trailblazer can later travel violently back through an authored Trace, striking things along it.

Goldline's combat should have a recognizable visual signature even with all UI removed.

---

# 24. Shield

The Colosseum shield becomes important.

Perfect blocks store force.

Stored force can be released as:

# RETURN

A gold shockwave.

---

# 25. Relics

Relics affect the videogame.

Never real business statistics.

Examples:

**Second Step** — perfect dodge leaves delayed offensive echo.

**Sixteenth Minute** — fatal damage briefly freezes time; defeat an enemy in that instant to survive.

**Sailmaker's Knot** — Linehook can pull lightweight enemies toward Trailblazer.

**Glass Fang** — much stronger heavy attacks at a defensive cost.

**Flood Pearl** — successful projectile dodge restores a small amount of health.

**Wrong Door** — discovering an optional dead end benefits movement during that expedition.

**Root Heart** — standing still regenerates slowly despite stillness being dangerous.

# 26. Death

Not:

GAME OVER.

The Cut reacts.

Gold Line catches Trailblazer.

Color drains.

Her form begins disintegrating.

The filament snaps tight.

She is violently pulled backward.

# RECOIL

She crashes into the most recent:

# ANCHOR

Characters may react.

Rook:

> “How was it?”

Trailblazer, smoking:

> “Short.”

# 27. Side stories

The world must contain things that do not explain the plot.

Possible examples:

* village terrorized by one highly intelligent goose;
* undefeated warrior begging Trailblazer to help him lose;
* ghost collecting rent without realizing she died;
* treasure hunter whose maps are always accurate but at the wrong scale;
* two towns accusing each other of stealing the moon;
* floating whale convinced the Wayward is its child;
* knight guarding a door for decades that opens into a cupboard;
* regional food everyone else thinks is disgusting;
* bizarre sports;
* fashion;
* pets;
* gambling;
* dumb laws;
* holidays;
* tourist traps.

The world does not know it is part of a videogame plot.

# 28. Cairn

Keep mystery.

Do not finish his full explanation too soon.

He is not a dark lord.

He looks like an old explorer.

Weathered clothing.

Ancient climbing gear.

Boots repaired over and over.

Makes tea.

Knows Trailblazer in ways he should not.

Possible first encounter:

Trailblazer approaches remote fire.

Without looking at her:

> “Still landing too hard on your left.”

That's enough.

# 29. White Stone / The Mercy

Cairn's visual language is not corruption.

It is **preservation**.

Perfect white stone.

Clean.

No cracks.

No flowers growing unexpectedly.

No mud.

No weathering.

No footprints.

Beautiful.

Wrong.

Eventually Trailblazer reaches:

# THE MERCY

No monsters.

No storms.

No disease.

Nothing rots.

Nothing breaks.

At first it seems wonderful.

Then:

no births.

No new songs.

No new construction.

Nobody leaves.

No unexpected art.

A child's drawing resembles every other child's drawing.

Do not explain what this means.

Let players notice.

# 30. Human test

Before adding anything:

**Would this be fun if the player never understands its metaphor?**

If no, reject it.

**Can the player describe the monster/place/character before explaining its symbolism?**

If no, symbolism is too exposed.

**Does the location have memorable social weirdness?**

If no, culture is unfinished.

**Does the protagonist read immediately in a phone recording?**

If no, composition failed.

**Does the environment make the player want to physically go somewhere?**

If no, stage framing failed.

**Could this screenshot be mistaken for a JRPG, Zelda-like, isometric RPG or side-scroller?**

If yes, reject the framing.

The deeper personal meaning exists beneath the adventure.

The player receives escapism first.
