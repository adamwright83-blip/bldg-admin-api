# Lantern City creative brief (V6 HUD direction)

Status: current as of 2026-09-08. Supersedes the informal "locked North Star"
writeups from the September concept images. Read this whole file before
proposing or implementing anything on the Lantern City surface at
`/growth/lantern-city`.

This brief exists so a fresh session can make decisions the way the product
owner would. It records the thesis, the hard rules, the screen hierarchy that
was approved, the parts of the concept pitch that were rejected and why, and
what already exists in the repo so you do not re-propose it.

---

## 1. The thesis

**The save file is the business.**

Goldline is not gamified operations software and must never be pitched or
framed that way. It is an actual game whose persistent world state is
generated by the real state of a pickup-and-delivery laundry business in Los
Angeles. Lantern City is that game's overworld.

The test of every feature: how much useful real-world work does the player
accomplish while subjectively feeling like they spent the day playing a game?

The player should spend an hour feeling like they reclaimed territory,
relit lanterns, rebuilt a broken machine, and pushed back an infestation, and
then close the app having actually called dormant customers, visited
buildings, won a new account, and grown recurring revenue.

**The immersion law.** No real-world task may ever be presented as a break
from the game. There is no "do some CRM work, then come back for your
reward." The work is the gameplay. There must be no psychological seam between
game action and business action.

**Who this is for.** One operator who hates thinking about the laundry
business and whose bottleneck is initiation: leaving the house, driving there,
walking in. Once in front of a human he is effective. Design for
executive-function friction: one clear next action, high emotional salience,
no shame mechanics, no to-do lists. The pitch's "males 30 to 55" framing is
irrelevant. The audience is this player.

---

## 2. Hard rules (non-negotiable, override every instinct)

1. **Never fabricate business truth.** No invented customers, revenue,
   meetings, contacts, leads, or geographic evidence. Every visual claim
   traces to an authoritative record.
2. **Effort is never upgraded to outcome.** A door knock does not animate like
   a paid order. The impact-class ladder is: observation, field_activity,
   response, opportunity, customer_outcome, economic_outcome. World
   transformation strength scales with the class actually reached.
3. **Real people stay real people.** Contacts are never rendered as creatures.
   A real customer is never given a synthetic portrait, invented dialogue,
   invented opinions, or invented facts. Psych signals attach to a situation,
   never to a person.
4. **Presentation state never flows into business state.** One direction only.
   Game actions never rewrite business-authored truth.
5. **Real data wins every conflict.**
6. **The world is persistent.** A helicopter that is 2 of 4 repaired today is 2
   of 4 tomorrow. Wins are permanent unless real business truth regresses.
   "The kingdom remembers."
7. **Authored copy is sparse.** The house voice is a line like "Someone
   answered the Line." Never a talking CRM. Never a talking HQ.
8. **Fictional waterways and invented geography are banned.** Real LA
   geography is authoritative.
9. **If it does not complete, it is not complete.** A mission is marked done
   only when the real event it represents has actually occurred.

**Banned outright:** fake XP, fake coins, arbitrary streaks, variable-reward
fake business events, simulated business events, a driving game, any Google
Maps substitute, screen interaction while driving, a talking HQ or voice-radio
handler, a GIS platform, layer-toggle checkbox panels, a population-management
sim, another analytics dashboard, generic SaaS cards, military-apocalypse
tropes, dark-mode misery, and any mechanic unrelated to a business outcome.

---

## 3. Why the direction changed

Earlier Lantern City builds were beautiful and illegible as a game. The player
could see neighborhoods, lanterns, counts, and fantasy objects, but the screen
still read as a dashboard wearing fantasy art. Specific failures:

- Territory chips said things like "Closed for Construction · 8" and "Healthy ·
  1 customer." Those are states with no verb. They do not tell the player what
  to do.
- A layer-toggle panel (checkboxes for lanterns, strongholds, labels) sat in the
  top right. That is a GIS control, and it was the most dashboard-shaped thing
  on screen.
- "Today's Quest: Your next chapter awaits" occupied the slot where the single
  next action belongs.
- Rat sprites were scattered over half the city as texture, so infestation
  stopped meaning anything.
- Objects that matter to gameplay (beacons, strongholds, frontier machines)
  were small and competed with the city art.

The V6 direction deliberately trades some painterly purity for game
readability. Within seconds of opening the map the player should know:
something is wrong somewhere, it can be fixed, there is one operation, progress
is measurable, completing it changes the world, and the city is responding.

**If it matters, the player must be able to see it.** Important objects get
visual authority at the full-map camera. One authoritative rat on the worst
district beats eleven decorative ones.

## 4. The approved screen hierarchy

Five zones. Each has one job. Do not let two zones become versions of the same
card.

### Top bar: real strategic numbers

Four to five figures, all real, each with a stated provenance. Approved shapes:

- Customers (count of real customer records)
- Districts lit (numerator over denominator, with the denominator being the
  authored V6 territory list)
- Weekly paid revenue (paid orders by paid-at date, Monday through business
  date)
- Dormant customers (dark cadence over total)
- Buildings secured (canonical strongholds with real occupancy)
- Recurring accounts (customers on a standing pickup schedule). This is the
  Defend metric.

Weekly deltas such as "+127 / wk" appear only with real week-over-week
provenance. Weather is never in the bar; real weather lives in the sky overlay.

Rejected: any scalar with no denominator or provenance. "IMPACT 8,930" is fake
XP. "TERRITORY 58%" is allowed only if the denominator is defined. A weather
widget is dashboard furniture.

### Left rail: the Featured Operation (mission control)

Answers "what am I doing right now?" Exactly one featured operation, not five
equal tasks. Other available operations may be collapsed beneath it.

Contents, in order: a strong-verb title, the territory, a short briefing
(one to three sentences, sparse, in the house voice), primary objectives with
real-ratio progress, a Launch Operation control that hands off into gameplay. On desktop, Launch
commits the operation and the Driver receives it; the action is done in the
field, never resolved inside this rail.

Objectives are truth-derived. Each is a ratio of real events:
- "RELIGHT <real customer name>" 0/1 (dormant customer places an order)
- "RESTORE DORMANT LIGHTS" 3/11 (dormant baseline at operation start, recovered
  since)
- "ESTABLISH THE SECOND LIGHT" 0/1 (a real new customer with first order after
  operation start, in this territory)

"Infestation pressure 73%" is not an objective until it is defined as a ratio
of real dormant recoveries. A percentage of nothing is banned.

### Center: the living city

The dominant visual surface. Real geography, painterly, lit by the real Los
Angeles day phase (`WorldDayPhase`). The September dusk concept is what 7 PM
looks like; marketing and default screenshots are daylight. Territory
chips carry a state and an action hint, not a state alone. "SILVER LAKE ·
INFESTED · 16 at risk" plus the operation verb beats "Infested · 16 customers."
"At risk" means customers in the dimming cadence state; state the definition
wherever the count appears. Frontier chips never say GUARDED or LOCKED (the
first echoes the retired Guardians, the second says keep out); they carry the
object's verb only: OPEN THE BALLOON, REBUILD THE BIRD.

### Right panel: Field Intel (the dossier)

Answers "who and what am I dealing with?" It is the intelligence layer for the
selected territory. It never repeats the mission card. Contents: district
state, real counts (total, dark, dimming, active), real revenue at risk, the
known light (a real dormant customer with real fields only: display name,
cadence state, last order date, order count, expected cadence), and the second
light slot (see section 5).

Rejected: an "Intelligence" paragraph of authored market analysis ("Silver
Lake is a high-potential market with strong demand"). That is a talking CRM.
If context is shown it is derived from records, not written.

Note: "Field Intel" is also the name of the approved voice-first capture flow
(Loot Walk). The panel may display that intel. It does not rename it.

### Bottom dock: primary game systems

Map, Missions, Customers, Buildings, Arsenal, Conquest. Already exists. A
secondary "new opportunity" alert may sit to the right of the dock when a real
opportunity record exists.

## 5. Characters: the ruling

The concept pitch proposed a character-driven mission system in the Metal Gear
Solid sense: operations are memorable because specific people are in them. The
diagnosis is correct. The execution was partly rejected.

### Real people (approved, with limits)

A real dormant customer can be the named target of an operation. "RELIGHT
ROGER" is legitimate because Roger is a real record the business needs to
recover. His dossier shows real fields only. What is banned for a real person:

- a synthetic or AI-generated portrait (their portrait is their lantern)
- invented quotes or opinions about them ("Good guy, just got busy")
- invented facts ("still lives in Silver Lake") unless a record says so
- any characterization beyond what the data supports

### The handler NPC (rejected)

"Marcus Vega, Field Operations," with a quote on every mission card, is the
talking HQ that was banned in the August brief, in text form. There is no
handler. The briefing text is sparse house voice with no attributed speaker.

### The fictional future customer (rejected as a person, approved as an object)

The pitch proposed "Elena," a named fictional prospect with a portrait and
backstory, standing in for "any new customer acquired in this territory," to be
replaced by the real person when one signs up.

Ruling: the need is real, the solution is wrong. A named fictional person with
a face and invented traits is a fabricated customer in every way that matters
psychologically. The player will go looking for her. It also breaks The
Answering, where reality is the random number generator: pre-naming the unknown
future customer turns the best surprise in the game into a scripted reveal.

The approved form is **The Second Light**: an unnamed dark lantern in the
territory, a place the player is trying to reach rather than a person they are
trying to find. It carries no name, no face, no traits. When a real new
customer places a first order in that territory, the lantern lights with their
real name and reality fills the slot. This gives specificity, a target, and a
persistent world object with zero counterfeit evidence.

**Fiction is allowed for the names of places, objects, machines, and
operations. Fiction is never allowed for people.**

## 6. Business-to-game grammar

Treat these as system mappings, not copywriting:

| Business                    | Game                              |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Sales                       | Territorial conquest              |
| Retention                   | Defense and stability             |
| Marketing                   | World expansion and operations    |
| Customers                   | Population and light              |
| Recurring customers         | Stability and power               |
| Buildings                   | Strongholds                       |
| Customer concentration      | Stronghold strength               |
| Dormancy and churn          | Decay and infestation             |
| Unworked markets            | Frontiers                         |
| Pipeline                    | Intelligence and opportunity      |
| Real-world actions          | Mission objectives                |
| Real results                | World transformation              |

The player should think "I want five more residents in OPUS" or "I don't want
Koreatown to fall." Those desires must produce exactly the real actions the
business needs.

## 7. Frontier objects and the impact ladder

The deflated balloon, broken helicopter, Wright-Brothers flight contraption,
and expedition equipment are promises of future capability in zero-customer
territories. They turn "we have nothing here" into "there is something here I
want." They stay visibly broken for days and repair one component per real
accomplishment.

Repair stages must map to the impact ladder. Rejected stage: "enter target
territory." Driving through a neighborhood reveals nothing and repairs nothing
(the Unwritten Map rule: only worked locations reveal). Acceptable stages, in
order: a worked visit to a qualified building (field_activity), a response from
a decision maker (response), a qualified opportunity (opportunity), a first
customer (customer_outcome).

---

## 8. Cause and effect

The player must repeatedly experience real action producing immediate world
consequence, scaled to the impact class actually reached:

- customer orders: lantern relights
- dormant customer recovered: infestation retreats
- first customer in a territory: frontier begins transforming, second light
  ignites
- new resident at a stronghold: the building visibly strengthens
- confirmed response from a building manager: a machine component repairs
- lost recurring customer: stability weakens, a lantern dims

Animation earns authority only from genuinely unseen real events. A refetch is
not an event. A cold first load replays nothing.

---

## 9. Tone and IP

Lantern City is the strategy overworld. Choosing an operation hands off into
the expedition heartbeat (ENTER THE LINE through SECURE CARGO), which is the
action game. Both feels are true: the map is Civilization and SimCity, the
mission is Tomb Raider II and Twisted Metal 2. If a mission resolves inside a
sidebar instead of on the ground, the action game is gone.

Protect the specific Goldline vocabulary over generic strategy furniture:
Lantern City, customer lanterns, architectural beacons, the OPUS giant golf
driver, the Century Park East valet bazooka, rat and cockroach infestation,
the broken helicopter, the deflated balloon, the flight contraption, expedition
objects, Tower Wars, territory reclamation, real customer geography, real
strongholds. Not soldiers, turrets, ammo, fuel, fictional cash, squads, mutant
bosses.

Mission titles use strong verbs: PURGE THE RAT NEST, RESTORE THE LIGHT,
REBUILD THE BIRD, OPEN THE BALLOON, LIGHT THE WESTSIDE, HOLD THE ROUTE. Never
"Contact Dormant Customers" or "Follow Up Leads."

Frontier districts are wanted, not forbidden. Padlock icons on zero-customer
territories say "keep out," which is the opposite of the intent. The broken
machine does the wanting job.

## 10. What already exists (do not re-propose)

On branch `lantern-city-finish-v6`, the V6 scene lives in
`client/src/components/admin/control-room/LanternCitySceneV6/`. The HUD
(`LanternCityHUD.tsx`) already renders a featured operation on the left and a
dossier on the right. The server derives the operation in
`server/goldlineWorld/lanternCityOverviewService.ts`, including:

- one featured operation per business date, titled by real environment state
  (PURGE THE RAT NEST when infested, RESTORE THE LIGHT otherwise, campaign
  titles when a campaign chapter is active)
- RELIGHT <real name> anchored to a real dormant customer
- RESTORE DORMANT LIGHTS as a ratio against the dormant baseline captured at
  operation start
- ESTABLISH THE SECOND LIGHT, which completes only when a real customer with a
  first order after operation start appears in the territory
- a scoreboard with provenance strings on every figure

Shared truth modules added 2026-09-08, each with tests, none yet rendered:

- `shared/lanternDecayForecast.ts` — "goes quiet in N days unless one order
  lands", pure cadence arithmetic run forward through the real visual-state
  reducer. Sparse cadence never forecasts. Horizon capped at 45 days.
- `shared/lanternFrontierCap.ts` — at most five visible frontier objects,
  priority: active campaign, lost ground, authored order. Overflow renders
  quiet with no object and no lock.
- `shared/lanternFrontierPresentation.ts` — `lostGroundKindForTerritory` now
  returns a different machine from the prize that departed. Wired into
  `composeLanternCityScene`.
- `shared/rekindlingArsenal.ts` — tool truth classes (system_sent, attested,
  redeemable), spark/ember/flame from the impact ladder, real cooldowns,
  Golden Seal cost, and a sent line that makes no promise.

Territory state art is registered to real neighborhood masks (not boxes).
Building identity is canonical across Home, Lantern City, and Tower Wars via
`buildingArt.ts`. The layer-toggle panel is gone.

The September concept images are the **layout authority**: five zones, one
featured operation, the dossier, the dock. This document is the **truth
authority**. Where they conflict (placeholder numbers, a weather widget, a
handler NPC, a fictional prospect, padlocks, GUARDED labels, an authored
district tagline, dusk as the default), this document wins. The Meta "tycoon"
light-mode concept (cash, reputation, citizens, power, water, materials,
mission rewards, upgrades, research, time-speed controls) is rejected in full;
only its daylight palette survives.

## 11. Decay, defence, and rescue (rulings added 2026-09-08)

1. **Decay advances only at nightly settlement.** Visual state is derived per
   business date, the same rule as Tower Wars folding. Nobody watches a rat
   appear at 3 PM. Improvement (a real order) may show immediately; decline
   only ever appears overnight, which is what makes the evening replay the
   moment the city changes.
2. **Decay is forecast, not just observed.** Field Intel carries one line:
   "Goes quiet in N days unless one order lands." Cadence arithmetic only;
   sparse cadence says nothing.
3. **Frontier cap of five visible objects, including lost ground.** Past the
   cap a territory is quiet and dark, not blockaded. Lost ground uses a
   distinct abandonment object, never the prize that already left.
4. **Rescue has two outs: rekindle or replace.** The building is the durable
   entity. A new resident in the same building counts as rescue.
5. **Defend means converting a customer to a standing recurring pickup.** A
   recurring customer's lantern is a hearth: heavier glass, slower to dim.
   Outreach to a healthy customer is spam, not defence.
6. **Arsenal tools carry truth classes and three states.** Sent is a spark,
   a real reply is an ember, an order is the flame. Cooldowns are real. The
   Golden Seal shows its cost. Copy never promises the light will answer.
7. **Infestation and every decay state are operator-private.** No public or
   consumer surface ever shows decay, lost ground, or a lock on previously
   held territory. Public surfaces show earned glow only.
8. **Blame is banned in copy.** Cooling, quiet, gone quiet, overgrown.
   Never "neglect". The art may keep rats and plywood; the words never say
   the operator did this.

## 12. Decision rules when forced to choose

- A beautiful dashboard or a legible game: the legible game.
- A fun fake progression mechanic or a real one: the real one.
- Generic commercial game language or Goldline's strange IP: Goldline.
- A named fictional person or an unnamed real-world slot: the slot.
- A talking handler or one sparse line: the line.
- Five tasks or one operation: one operation.
- A prettier outcome or the true one: the true one.

The objective is not to make admin.bldg.chat look like a Steam game. It is to
make the business operating system genuinely play like one, while every number
on screen stays true.
